Here is a description of what we were investigating in this experiment:

Perceptual Learning

This study is part of a larger project designed to test what is called 'perceptual learning'. Previous studies have shown that, when presented with words from speakers who have a slightly different speech pattern, people are able to adjust their perception of sounds to match the shifted input. This process of accomodation may be behind how the sound patterns of language change over time.

To test this perceptual learning process further, we needed to create words which are slightly different from what people would normally hear. First, we prepared a set of sounds that are between an 's' and an 'f' by mixing original recordings of these two together. Other participants categorized whether they heard these sounds as an 's' or an 'f', allowing us to find the maximally ambiguous blend; that is, the sound that people can hear as either an 's' or 'f' depending on the context. This ambiguous sound was then inserted into words which normally end in an 'f' sound.

In the experiment you just completed, you were first asked to identify whether sounds were 's' and 'f'. This establishes for us your baseline perception of these two sounds. Then, by listening to a set of words and non-words, we presented the ambiguous sound described above to you. We expect these sounds to shift your perception of the speaker's 'f' sounds. We measure this change in the final categorization phase. The amount that your perception does shift will then be compared to other participants with different experimental conditions, for example multiple speakers or different sounds.
